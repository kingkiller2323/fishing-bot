## 1. Headline

<!-- generated:summary-headline -->
<!-- /generated:summary-headline -->

What changes for a player:

- **Pacing.** Hours to Lv 20 stay close to today's, even though the curve's XP at Lv 20 rises. The extra quest and streak XP is absorbed (§2). After Lv 20 each level costs more on a smooth quartic curve, and it replaces today's cliff (Lv 20→50 in under three hours of play). Every gear tier is bought during the stage before its level and equipped on arrival, so a better rod never blocks a level (§6).
- **Catch volume.** A crafted rod no longer lands up to 15 fish. The normal catch averages from 1.1 (a Common crafted rod) to 1.8 fish per cast. The reference tiers T1–T5 are 1.2–1.8, with occasional 3–5 fish jackpots (decision 2). A value model and XP per rarity carry the difference (§6.4).
- **Money.** Income comes from fishing, plus quest, streak and buff cash. It pays for repairs (small, predictable upkeep), one crate assembly per rod tier and one-time biome permits (§7). The optional spending:
  - XP bait and the aquarium lower savings.
  - Money bait is a gross sink but a small net source.
  - Display tanks are the aspirational sink.

  Players can save (decision 10), and in the reference loop they keep most of their income (§13, `P-SAVINGS`).
- **Daily play.** Top.gg voting is retired. A DCC-native streak (Streak Crate / Chest) and typed daily/weekly quests let a casual player keep pace per hour played (§5, §9, §10). A player who logs in only for the daily minimum never overtakes an engaged player (R2).
- **Founder.** Fewer visible fish, the same power through private multipliers, a public level from base XP, and every Founder catch non-competitive. The Founder is still identifiable unless you choose the stealth option (§14).

### 15.6 Rods and bait (details in the module docs)

- **C1 (rods):** repairing a crafted rod charges money and repairs nothing. The update goes through the wrong Mongoose discriminator and matches no document. Reproduced on the in-memory MongoDB. This is a correctness fix on today's numbers. Refunds are decision `P-RODS-C1-REFUND`.

## 18. After approval: implementation order

1. **Urgent hotfixes (§15.0)**, each isolated and tested, once you approve them.
2. **Correctness fixes on today's numbers** (§15), each with tests.
3. **The balance release:**
   - engine rules: the curve with its no-demotion guard, the rod converter and multi-catch chain, the value model and XP per rarity, per-cast bait, typed quests, the streak, buffs (B1 + B2 with catch-time Double Cash), permits and grandfathering, aquarium rules, the Founder rules (`P-FOUNDER-*`);
   - the exploit closures that need values;
   - the approved numbers, loaded from one balance version.
4. **Post-release telemetry:**
   - XP per active hour by play pattern, at equal level (`P-DAILY-FACTOR`);
   - savings rate (`P-SAVINGS`);
   - bait use;
   - buff income share.

**Rules for every implementation step:**
- **Migrations** are additive and idempotent: new fields and marker-guarded backfills, never a wipe of a user or progression collection.
- **Catalog changes** (Fishing Crate price and level, Old Rod `unbreakable`, bait, licences, quests) go through a `catalogRevision`-guarded sync. The seed only inserts missing rows.
- **Level reads and writes** all go through `max(stored, curve)` at the curve change. That includes the public level, for every player (`P-CURVE-EXISTING`, `P-FOUNDER-PUBLIC-LEVEL`).
- **Pending cast and box journals** written by pre-5B code must replay under the new code:
  - `writeCast` and `applyGachaResult` accept the old result shape, with a test like the hotfix's;
  - recovery is isolated per journal, so one bad journal cannot stop the bot from starting.
- **Approval.** Each step stops for your approval before it touches production.
