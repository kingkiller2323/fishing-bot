1. **The value model holds across biomes and gear.** Income rises strictly with biome at every tier and access level, and never falls with tier within a biome. XP per cast doesn't depend on the biome. Today's inverted ladder (River best, Coast worst) is gone (§2).
2. **Biome permits are one-time purchases alongside the level requirement; Ocean is free.** A permit costs a fixed share of what the reference (regular) player is expected to earn fishing the stage before it (§3.2). The prices are in the table above and in §3.3.
3. **Permits are never a second wall.** On the integrated reference loop every archetype owns every permit the moment it reaches the level, with a large margin (§3.4). That still holds on fish income alone, and with part of that income spent elsewhere (§3.5). Permits don't delay any rod tier beyond a fraction of a session, and they don't change any level time. The R1 record is reproduced exactly (§3.7).
4. **Grandfathering is additive and idempotent.** An account without a `permits` field receives a `grandfathered` permit for every live biome it already qualifies for, plus its current biome. Nobody loses access (§4).
5. **Mountain Stream** is the first expansion biome, designed but not implemented. It is a freshwater ladder in which the weather decides the premium tier, with one strong Ultra salmon per weather. Today's biome is unreachable, and would mostly fail to catch even if it could be reached (§5.1). Its base value is the framework's proposed value (`P-MS-VALUE`), which keeps its step over Swamp inside the live ladder's band (§5.4).
6. **Post-50.** The 50 → 60 stage is the longest integrated stage. It buys the endgame rod tier and the Mountain Stream permit, and the time per level rises smoothly with no wall (§6). Everything after 60 is a curve-based sketch outside the integrated model (§6, §7).

## 3. Biome permits

### 3.1 Rules
- **Access:** a player can fish a biome when their **gate level** ≥ the biome's level **and** they own its permit. Ocean is free.
  - The gate level is the level itself for normal players, and the public level for the Founder (`P-FOUNDER-GATE`).
  - One pure helper, `accessibleBiomes(level, permits)`, is shared by `/biome`, the cast guard and the Streak Crate's `'highestUnlocked'` pool.
- **Buying:**
  - A permit can be bought once the gate level reaches the biome's level.
  - It is a one-time purchase: account-bound, never consumed, never expires and not refundable.
  - It does **not** require the previous biome's permit, so each permit is an independent milestone.
  - It is bought from `/biome` (a "Buy permit ($X)" button on a biome the player has the level for), in one guarded atomic update.
- **Visibility:**
  - The level-up line announces the new biome and its permit price ("Lake unlocked · permit $X · /biome").
  - `/biome` lists each biome as "Owned", "Permit $X" or "Requires Lv N".
- **Price:** the same for everyone. A permit is a cost, not a reward, so there is no base/final split. The Founder pays the same price from final cash.
- **Competitive:** permits don't affect `competitiveEligible`.
- **Sink category (decision 10):** a **progression purchase**, not upkeep. Nothing recurs. A player who doesn't buy keeps fishing the biome below and keeps their money.

### 3.2 Price formula (`permitPrice(biome)`)

```
permit(k) = nicePrice( s × E(k) ),   s = PARAMS.permits.stageShare,   PARAMS.priceSigDigits significant digits
E(k)      = stageHours_ref(level(k−1) → level(k)) × $/h( biome k−1, F.typicalTier(k−1), reference cadence )
```

- `stageHours_ref` is the reference player's (`F.REFERENCE_ARCHETYPE`) hours of play in the stage. It comes from the **integrated reference loop without permits**, with every live biome open at its level (`referenceStages()`).
  - The permit table shows E(k) next to the fish income the integrated run actually earned in the stage. The two agree, so the formula prices what the player really earns.
- **Equivalently:** permit(k) = H(k) hours of the income the player earns just before the level, with H(k) = s × stage hours.
- **Why a stage share rather than a flat number of hours:**
  - Stages grow steeply from the first to the last (§6.2).
  - A flat H would be either punitive early (more than the whole first stage) or trivial late.
  - The share scales with both stage length and stage income, and it regenerates with the framework: a change to the curve, the gear path or the daily systems reprices the permits mechanically.

**Why this share** (`P-WORLD-PERMIT-SHARE`):
- **Affordability does not bind** anywhere in the sweep, even under the harshest stress. The share is therefore a question of feel, not of access.
- **At the proposed share a permit reads as a milestone:**
  - it costs a noticeable, growing slice of the income the player earns just before the level;
  - it pays itself back within a few hours in the new biome (§3.3);
  - it stays a small share of lifetime income (§3.6).
- **A higher share would also be affordable** but moves toward a money sink. Permits aren't meant to solve accumulation (§12), so that is a user decision, not a model constraint.
- The design-stage argument, that the share was capped by casual players' fish-only delays, no longer holds. On the integrated core a permit due at the level ranks ahead of the next rod tier (§8), and the daily systems add cash. Saving for rods no longer starves the permit.

## 4. Grandfathering and migration

**Rule** (`grandfatheredPermits(user)`; pure; `P-WORLD-GRANDFATHER`):
- An account **without** a `permits` field receives a `grandfathered` permit record for:
  - every live non-free biome with `BIOME_LEVEL ≤ max(stored level, today's levelForXp(xp))`;
  - **plus its current biome**, so nobody is moved out of the water they're standing in.
- An account that already has the field, even `[]`, is untouched.

**Record shape:** `{ biome, source: 'grandfathered' | 'purchased', acquiredAt, pricePaid }`.

**Level used:** "already qualifies" means today's rule, `max(stored, 100·L² curve)`. The 5B curve never lowers a level (level = max(stored, curve)), so grandfathering by today's level is the most generous correct basis, whether it runs before or after the curve change.

## 8. Integration contract (the world as a system on the shared core)

`world.system(opts)` returns a fresh `lifecycle.js` system per call; `integrate.js` composes it as `'world'` in `REFERENCE`. All per-run state lives in `state.sys.world`. The full contract is in `report().integration`.

**Hooks:**
- `init`: permits held from the start. Ocean is free. Grandfathered players get every live biome at or below `opts.grandfatheredLevel`, or the biomes in `opts.grandfathered` (names or `grandfatheredPermits()` records).
- `canFish(biome)`: a **live** biome whose permit is held. With `opts.permits: false`, every live biome is open at its level; this is the reference-stage model behind `permitPrice()`. Mountain Stream is never fishable; `opts.expansion` only offers its permit.
- `goals`: one goal per unowned permit whose level the **gate level** has reached. Category `'progression'`, item `'permit:<biome>'`, cost `permitPriceMap()` (or `opts.prices`). A permit the player can't afford yet blocks the goals after it.
- `on('levelUp')` of the gate kind (public under the Founder's public gate) records when each biome level is reached. `onDayStart`/`onDayEnd` flag purchases made in the core's end-of-day purchase pass.

**Purchase order (`permitPriority()`, differs from `LC.PRIORITY`):**
- The design funds goals in order of the level they're due at, and a tie goes to the rod (`PARAMS.schedule.tieBreak = 'rod-first'`).
- A permit due before the pending rod tier takes `LC.PRIORITY.permit`. Otherwise it goes right after the rod (`LC.PRIORITY.rod + 1`).
- `'permit-first'` (sensitivity) moves ties ahead of the rod. `opts.priority` sets a fixed priority.

**Ledger:**
- The world grants nothing: no XP or cash source.
- Spend: `progression` → `permit:<biome>` for each live non-free biome, plus `permit:Mountain Stream` with `opts.expansion`.

**Helpers:**
- `permitSummary(result)`: time to afford per permit from any `simulate()` result (reached, bought, delay in hours and sessions, money held at the level ÷ price).
- `lifecycle(archetype, { config, permits, share, prices, expansion, grandfatheredLevel, stopAtLevel })`: one integrated run of a `CONFIGS` configuration with the world system. It is the only way this module runs a lifecycle.

**The retired private loop.** `world.lifecycle()` used to run its own time-stepping loop (curve.js-style XP with the provisional daily XP, rods paid on schedule). That loop was deleted at this migration, together with its validation baselines and its `curve.json` replication check; the function now runs the integrated model. ## 10. Migrations (additive and idempotent only)

**`migrateBiomePermits()`** runs at bootstrap, before login, following the `migrateAutoLockSpecies` pattern:

```
for user in User.find({ permits: { $exists: false } }).select('userId level xp currentBiome'):
    permits = grandfatheredPermits(user, { now })      // world.js rule, baked into engine/world.js
    User.updateOne({ _id: user._id, permits: { $exists: false } }, { $set: { permits } })
```

- It only adds a missing field, and never touches `level`, `xp`, money or `currentBiome`.

## 12. Risks
- **Accumulation is not the world's to solve.** On the integrated loop players keep most of their income to Lv 60, and permits are a small share of it (§3.6). Permits are a progression milestone, not a sink target. Expansion biomes, their gear and the aspirational sinks carry that (decision 10).
  - Raising the stage share would be affordable (§3.5), but it would turn permits into a sink. That is a user decision.
- **Prices move with the framework.** They depend on the integrated reference stage hours: the curve, the gear path, and the quests/streak/buffs XP.
  - Bake them into `balance.js` only at the final framework version.
  - `P-WORLD-PERMIT-PRICES` pins today's values, so `decisions.verify()` fails loudly if they drift.
- **"A second gate" perception.** Mitigations:
  - no modelled delay;
  - the price is announced at level-up;
  - one-click purchase in `/biome`;
  - no prerequisite chain;
  - grandfathering.
- **Existing players are rich.** Permits above their grandfathered set will be trivial for them for a while. That is harmless: a permit is a milestone, not a sink.
- **Post-60 figures are a sketch.** Mountain Stream is priced per cast (the mirror has exact parity with the framework on every live biome). The post-60 hours are curve-based and outside the integrated model. The real ladder should be re-measured with `measure.js` once seeded.
